## The Question Type You Were Describing

You nailed the description. Those are called **Variable Relationship** questions (some prep courses also file them under **Analyzing Functions**). They're the type most study guides skip, and they're a real chunk of the exam.

The underlying math has real names:

1. **Sign analysis** — reasoning about whether a result is positive or negative based on the signs of its parts. No numbers needed.
2. **Direct, inverse, joint, and combined variation** — how one variable moves when another moves.
   - Direct: `y = kx` → x up, y up
   - Inverse: `y = k/x` → x up, y down
   - Joint: `y = kxz` → depends on both
   - Combined: `y = kx/z` → x up = y up, z up = y down
3. **Qualitative / covariational reasoning** — "if x increases, what happens to y?" with no numbers given.

### The 4 rules that solve almost all of them

**Rule 1 — Sign multiplication/division:**
- pos × pos = pos
- neg × neg = pos
- pos × neg = neg
- Same rules for division.
- **An even exponent kills the sign** (x² is always positive if x ≠ 0). **An odd exponent keeps it** (x³ keeps x's sign).

**Rule 2 — Top vs. bottom.** In a fraction, anything on **top** moves the answer the **same** direction. Anything on the **bottom** moves it the **opposite** direction.

> In `R = kL / A`: L doubles → R doubles. A doubles → R halves.

**Rule 3 — Exponents multiply the effect.** In `P = I²R`, doubling I doesn't double P — it multiplies P by 2² = **4**. Halving I gives 1/4.

**Rule 4 — When stuck, plug in easy numbers.** Pick x = 2, then x = −2, then x = 1/2. Fastest way to break a trick question. Use this as your fallback — don't burn 3 minutes staring at it.

**The traps to watch for:**
- Squares hide signs. `y = x²` with a *negative* x that gets *more* negative → y goes **up**.
- Numbers between 0 and 1 behave backwards. If 0 < x < 1, then x² is **smaller** than x.
- A shrinking denominator makes a **huge** result.
- "Cannot be determined" is sometimes genuinely correct — but it's also the bait answer. Check whether an even exponent or an unknown sign really blocks you.

# ANSWER KEY & EXPLANATIONS

## Part 1

**1. C — 159.** Rule: ×2 + 1. (79 × 2) + 1 = 159.

**2. D — 720.** Multiply by increasing whole numbers: ×2, ×3, ×4, ×5, ×6. 120 × 6 = 720.

**3. B — 6.** Divide by 2 each time.

**4. C — 30.** Differences are 1, 3, 5, 7 (consecutive odds). Next difference is 9. 21 + 9 = 30.

**5. C — 21.** Alternating pattern: −3, +6, −3, +6, −3. Next is +6. 15 + 6 = 21.

**6. C — 17.** These are prime numbers. Next prime after 13 is 17. (15 is 3×5, so it's out.)

**7. C — 6.** 7x − 4 = 3x + 20 → 4x = 24 → x = 6.

**8. B — 4.** 5x + 15 − 2x + 8 = 35 → 3x + 23 = 35 → 3x = 12 → x = 4. *Watch the minus sign distributing across (x − 4): it becomes −2x + 8, not −2x − 8.*

**9. B — C/(2π).** Divide both sides by 2π.

**10. C — 41.** 3(−3)² − 4(−3) + 2 = 3(9) + 12 + 2 = 27 + 12 + 2 = 41. *The square makes it +9, and −4 times −3 is +12. Two sign traps in one problem.*

**11. B — 2(x + 3)(x − 3).** Pull out the 2 first, then difference of squares on x² − 9.

**12. A — x + 3.** x² + 7x + 12 factors to (x + 3)(x + 4). The (x + 4) cancels.

**13. A — −5 and 3.** Need two numbers multiplying to −15 and adding to +2: that's +5 and −3. So (x + 5)(x − 3) = 0, giving x = −5 and x = 3. *Note the roots are the opposite signs of the factors.*

**14. A — 12x⁵y⁶.** Multiply coefficients (4 × 3 = 12), add exponents (x³·x² = x⁵, y²·y⁴ = y⁶).

**15. C — 27x⁶y³.** The outside exponent hits everything: 3³ = 27, (x²)³ = x⁶, y³.

**16. C — 25.** (2/5)x = 10 → x = 10 × 5/2 = 25.

**17. A — 2.** Add the two equations: the 2y and −2y cancel, giving 4x = 16, x = 4. Then 4 − 2y = 0 → y = 2. *The question asks for y, not x — an easy point to throw away.*

**18. C — 7.** Cross-multiply: 6(x − 1) = 3(x + 5) → 6x − 6 = 3x + 15 → 3x = 21 → x = 7.

**19. B — 15.** Square both sides: 3x + 4 = 49 → 3x = 45 → x = 15.

**20. A — −2.** (−7 − 5) / (4 − (−2)) = −12 / 6 = −2.

**21. B — (2, 0).** Set y = 0: 0 = −3x + 6 → 3x = 6 → x = 2.

**22. C.** Parallel lines have identical slopes. Only C keeps the 2/3.

**23. B — $1,568.** 40 × $32 = $1,280. Overtime rate is $48/hr; 6 hrs × $48 = $288. Total $1,568.

**24. B — 36 ft.** (3 × 46) + (2 × 38) = 138 + 76 = 214. 250 − 214 = 36.

**25. B — 6 hours.** Add the rates, not the times: 1/10 + 1/15 = 3/30 + 2/30 = 5/30 = 1/6 of the job per hour → 6 hours. *Never average the two times. That's the trap answer (12.5).*

**26. C — 200.** 3 + 5 = 8 parts. 320 ÷ 8 = 40 per part. The 3/4-inch is 5 parts: 5 × 40 = 200.

**27. A — $10,540.** Under budget means you pay 85%. 12,400 × 0.85 = 10,540. *Shortcut: 10% is 1,240 and 5% is 620, so subtract 1,860.*

**28. B — 10 days.** Find the per-person-per-day rate: 120 ÷ (3 × 5) = 8 fixtures per apprentice per day. Five apprentices = 40/day. 400 ÷ 40 = 10 days.

**29. C — 60 mph.** 2 hr 45 min = 2.75 hours. 165 ÷ 2.75 = 60.

**30. C — multiplied by 4.** I is squared, so doubling it multiplies P by 2² = 4. **This is Rule 3.**

**31. B — negative.** Positive result from a negative times something means the something must be negative. **Rule 1.**

**32. D — unchanged.** L is on top (doubling it doubles R) and A is on the bottom (doubling it halves R). The two effects cancel. **Rule 2.** This is exactly the kind of question you described.

**33. B — decreases.** The x term is negative, so as x grows, you subtract more, and y falls.

## Part 2 — Variable Relationships

**V1. A — positive.** Negative × negative = positive.

**V2. B — negative.** A negative minus a positive moves further negative. (Try −5 − 3 = −8.)

**V3. B — negative.** Positive ÷ something = negative means the divisor is negative.

**V4. B — negative.** m² is positive no matter what m is (since m ≠ 0). Positive × negative = negative. **The even exponent erases m's sign entirely — that's the whole point of the question.**

**V5. B — negative.** Test both cases. If b is positive: ab < 0 forces a negative, and bc > 0 forces c positive → ac is negative. If b is negative: a is positive and c is negative → ac is still negative. Either way, negative.

**V6. C — negative and larger in absolute value than m.** To drag a positive m down past zero, n has to be negative *and* bigger. (3 + n = −2 requires n = −5.)

**V7. D — √x.** With fractions between 0 and 1, raising to a power makes them *smaller*, and taking a root makes them *bigger*. Plug in x = 1/4: x² = 1/16, x³ = 1/64, √x = 1/2. **Classic trap — the instinct is to pick x³.**

**V8. B — x².** The square makes it positive; everything else stays negative. Test x = −2: x = −2, x² = 4, x³ = −8.

**V9. C — divided by 3.** x is on the bottom, so tripling it cuts y to a third. Inverse variation.

**V10. C — multiplied by 4.** x is on the bottom *and* squared. Halving x means dividing the denominator by 4, which multiplies y by 4.

**V11. B — decreases.** Rearrange to a = F/m. m on the bottom, so bigger m means smaller a.

**V12. A — increases.** Smaller denominator, bigger result. **Rule 2.**

**V13. B — decreases.** I = V/R. R on the bottom.

**V14. C — unchanged.** Doubling x doubles Q; halving y cuts it back in half. They cancel exactly.

**V15. A — increases.** This is the trickiest one here. x going from −2 to −5 means x² goes from 4 to 25. The value of x *decreased*, but y *increased*, because the square strips the sign. **If you missed this one, it's the exact type you described running into.**

**V16. B — less than bc.** Multiplying an inequality by a negative flips it. (4 > 2, but 4(−1) = −4 which is less than 2(−1) = −2.)

**V17. A — increases.** As the denominator shrinks toward zero, the result blows up. Try 3/0.1 = 30, then 3/0.01 = 300.

**V18. C — either positive or negative.** Both +3 and −3 square to 9. The square destroyed the information about the sign, so you genuinely can't recover it. **This is a case where "cannot be determined" logic is the right answer, not the bait.**

**V19. C — divided by 4.** c² is positive regardless of c's sign, so the negative is irrelevant — that's the distractor. Doubling |c| multiplies c² by 4, and c² is on the bottom, so d is divided by 4. The sign of d does not change.

**V20. A — positive.** p × q is negative × negative = positive. For the whole product to stay positive, r must be positive.

## Part 3 — Bonus

**B1. C — 96 sq ft.** 12 × 8.

**B2. B — 10.** 6-8-10 is a 3-4-5 triangle doubled. Worth memorizing 3-4-5 and 5-12-13.

**B3. B — 15 ft.** 9² + 12² = 81 + 144 = 225. √225 = 15. (Another 3-4-5, tripled.)

**B4. C — 44 in.** C = πd = (22/7)(14) = 44.

**B5. C — 60 cu ft.** 3 × 4 × 5.

**B6. B — 78.5 sq in.** A = πr² = 3.14 × 25 = 78.5. *Don't use the diameter formula here — that's what the 157 distractor is for.*

**B7. C — 200 rpm.** Gears are inversely related: half the teeth means twice the speed.

**B8. B — 50 lb.** Load × load arm = effort × effort arm. 200 × 3 = 600. 600 ÷ 12 = 50.

**B9. B — 50 lb.** A single movable pulley gives a 2:1 mechanical advantage.

2. **Drill Part 2 until the sign rules are automatic.** Every one of those should take under 30 seconds once the four rules are reflex.
3. **Practice everything without a calculator.** Fractions, percents, long division, squares up to 15². Your speed here is the whole game.
4. **Memorize:** squares 1–15, the 3-4-5 and 5-12-13 triangles, common fraction-to-decimal conversions (1/8 = 0.125, 3/8 = 0.375, etc.).
