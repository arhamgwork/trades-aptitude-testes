The four rules behind sign analysis

These questions ask which direction something moves, not what it equals. Standard prep guides skip them and they show up in number series, problem solving, and mechanical reasoning alike. Learn the four rules and they stop being guesswork.

1. Multiply on the same side, move the same way

If two quantities are multiplied together to make a third, raising either one raises the result. Lowering either one lowers it. Direct relationship.

A = L × W  →  L ↑ (W fixed) → A ↑

2. Anything under the bar moves the opposite way

A quantity in the denominator is inverse. Raising it lowers the result. This is the rule people get backwards under time pressure — check where the variable sits before you answer.

P = F ÷ A  →  A ↑ (F fixed) → P ↓

3. Hold the result still and the other two trade off

When the answer is fixed and one input rises, the other input must move the opposite way to compensate. This is how "flow stays constant, pipe gets smaller" questions work.

Q = V × A, Q fixed  →  A ↓ → V ↑

4. Count the negatives, then chain the arrows

In a product or quotient, an odd number of negative factors gives a negative result and an even number gives a positive one. In a chain of relationships, follow each arrow in order — two reversals put you back where you started.

A ↑ → B ↓ → C ↓  ⟹  A ↑ gives C ↓
